Letters to the future: what to write to your child

Five opening lines that solve the blank page.

Start with one of these

  • Today you did something I want you to know about...
  • One thing I am afraid of forgetting is...
  • If I could tell you what it is like being your mother (or your father) today, I would say...
  • By the time you read this you will be X years old, and I wanted you to know...
  • Today was a hard day, and even so...

It does not have to be beautiful

The letters that move people most are not the well written ones: they are the ones that tell something specific. "You cried all night and I cried with you at four in the morning" is worth more than any paragraph about unconditional love. Write it badly, write it short, but write it concrete.

Seal it

When you file it away, choose an opening date: her fifteenth birthday, her eighteenth. A letter she knows exists and cannot read yet is a present that lasts years before it is opened.

Keep it in My Baby

Write the letter and choose the day it opens. Until then, not even you can open, edit or delete it.

Document one whole ordinary day

Pick any Saturday and photograph it hour by hour, staging nothing. A day with nothing special about it is the one you will most want to see again. Everyday life

The mark on the wall

A door frame, a pencil and the date. It is the most analogue record there is and the one that moves people most when the family moves house. Photograph it before you go.
