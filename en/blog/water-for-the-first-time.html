Water for the first time

The sea, a pool, a stream or a basin in the garden. The face when a foot touches cold water is a photo you only get to take once.

Before leaving the house

  • Check with whoever looks after her health about sunscreen and sun exposure, which changes with age.
  • Go early in the morning or late in the afternoon. Midday sun is uncomfortable and the water gets too warm.
  • Take more clothes than seems necessary: you will be changing her three times.
  • A large hooded towel, a wide-brimmed hat and guaranteed shade, a parasol or a tent.

The first reaction is usually not a smile

A fright, crying and clinging to your neck are all common reactions. It does not mean she dislikes water; it means this is a new and enormous sensation. Go in holding her, slowly, wetting her feet first.

Recording it without missing it

  • Ask someone else to take the photos. Both your hands will be busy.
  • The photo that matters is the face at the instant of first contact, and it lasts two seconds.
  • Film fifteen seconds with sound: the noise of the water with her squeal is half the memory.
  • One photo of the feet in the sand or the water, from above. Simple and always lovely.

Afterwards

Write two lines the same day: where it was, how old she was, and how she really reacted. Ten years from now you will remember that there was a first time, not what it was like.
